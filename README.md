# Bayou Broth — Cajun × Ramen Mobile Noodle House

Static GitHub Pages concept for **Bayou Broth**, a Cajun × ramen food truck / mobile noodle house. The product direction is food-first, route-first, and distinctly Gulf South without turning into a themed caricature.

> **Current brand name:** Bayou Broth  
> **Category:** Cajun × ramen fusion food truck  
> **Positioning:** Louisiana pantry + ramen technique  
> **Experience:** bold, practical, street-level, warm, memorable

## Customer journey

**Brand hero / next drop → weekly route → signature bowl → fusion story → menu → street service → catering**

The website should answer the highest-value customer questions quickly:
- Where is the truck today?
- What is on the menu?
- What makes the food different?
- How much does it cost?
- Can I book the truck for an event?

## Brand direction

Bayou Broth should feel like a **real independent food truck with a strong local identity**, not an AI-generated restaurant concept and not a luxury ramen bar.

### Visual language
- Swamp green, black/ink, weathered cream, Cajun red, saffron/gold accents
- Bold condensed display type with straightforward utility text
- Editorial street-food composition rather than polished SaaS cards
- Louisiana references used sparingly: cypress silhouettes, marsh texture, fleur-de-lis geometry, hand-painted truck graphics, butcher-paper / menu-board details
- Japanese influence should come primarily through ramen technique, bowl presentation, chopsticks, noodle forms, and disciplined composition—not generic Asian décor
- Surfaces should feel tactile: black melamine bowls, stainless food-truck counters, butcher paper, worn wood, paper napkins, condiment bottles, chalkboards, real pavement

## Photography direction — realistic first

All new photography should resemble **documentary restaurant and food-truck photography**, not stylized AI fantasy.

### Food photography rules
- Photograph plausible, eatable bowls with believable portion sizes and ingredient placement
- Favor 35–50mm environmental food shots and 70–100mm detail shots
- Use shallow but not extreme depth of field; enough of the bowl should remain sharp
- Use one believable key light source: window light, service-window light, or a soft diffused lamp
- Keep white balance natural; warm atmosphere is welcome, but food colors should remain true
- Use steam sparingly and naturally
- Preserve small imperfections: broth splashes, asymmetrical garnish, fingerprints on a bowl rim, slightly uneven noodles, real condensation
- Avoid excessive orange glow, impossible shine, oversized ingredients, hyper-detailed garnish piles, perfect symmetry, fake smoke, floating props, and fantasy backgrounds

### Signature bowl direction
A believable Cajun-ramen bowl may combine:
- pork or chicken ramen broth
- ramen noodles
- chashu or smoked pork
- sliced andouille
- jammy egg
- scallion
- black garlic / chili crisp
- optional okra or charred corn in restrained amounts

The bowl should still read as ramen first, with Louisiana ingredients integrated naturally.

### Food-truck photography rules
- Show the truck doing actual work: service window open, cook plating, customer receiving a bowl, queue forming, condiment station, night-market context
- Favor dusk, early evening, overcast daylight, or practical service-window lighting
- Realistic stainless-steel interiors and normal food-truck clutter are desirable
- Customers should look candid rather than posed
- Branding should appear as painted/vinyl truck graphics, menu boards, stickers, paper goods, or apparel—not pristine floating logos
- Keep crowds modest and believable
- Do not fill every surface with brand graphics or slogans

## Final image shot list

1. **Signature bowl / website feature** — 3/4 angle, believable Cajun-ramen bowl, dark bowl, simple wood or stainless setting, natural side light.
2. **Hero environment** — Bayou Broth truck during service at dusk, open service window, 2–5 customers, restrained branding, food activity visible.
3. **Service moment** — cook handing one bowl through the truck window to a customer; documentary framing.
4. **Noodle detail** — chopsticks lifting noodles from a real bowl, shallow depth of field, no dramatic fantasy steam.
5. **Prep/detail** — andouille being seared, scallions, chili crisp, broth pot, or plated egg inside a working truck kitchen.
6. **Table/lifestyle** — one or two finished bowls on a picnic table with napkins, drink, and ordinary food-truck context.
